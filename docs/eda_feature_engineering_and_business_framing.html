Executive Summary

This stage moves from data cleaning into exploratory analysis, feature engineering and a first price model. Asking prices are heavily right-skewed, so the analysis works on the log of price. Vehicle age and miles-per-year were engineered as new features and brands were grouped into Luxury and Standard tiers. A linear regression on log(price) reached an R² of 0.58 on held-out listings. Vehicle age is the strongest price driver: each extra year lowers the asking price by about 6.8%, and every 10,000 miles by about 3.6%, with body type, fuel, drive and brand tier held fixed.

The log scale expands the dense low-price region and compresses extreme high-end outliers, giving equal modeling weight to all price tiers.

On the log scale the spread is more even, and the model treats a 10% price change consistently whether the base price is $5,000 or $50,000.

A5 Feature Engineering
Engineered Features
FeatureLogicRationale
vehicle_age 2021 − model year (minimum 0) Converts model year into an interpretable depreciation signal. The reference year is 2021 because every ad was posted in April–May 2021, so this is the age at listing.
miles_per_year odometer ÷ max(vehicle_age, 1) Normalizes usage intensity across ages. Floor of 1 applied to avoid division-by-zero for new vehicles (age = 0).
brand_segment Luxury / Standard classification Groups manufacturers into two market tiers using a fixed, explicit list, reducing cardinality while preserving a brand value signal.

Encoding Decisions
Model column: Top 20 most frequent models retained; all remaining grouped as "Other". Prevents feature explosion (the column has about 24,000 distinct strings) while preserving signal for high-volume models.

Low-cardinality columns (body type, fuel, transmission, drive, brand segment): Encoded via one-hot encoding — with the most common value as the baseline, so every coefficient reads as a difference from a typical car.

Model Conclusions
Age Dominates
Removing vehicle age drops the model's R² from 0.58 to 0.44 — more than three times the effect of removing odometer, the next most important feature.
Truck & Off-Road Premium
Compared with a sedan of the same age and mileage, pickups are listed about 65% higher and off-road vehicles about 70% higher. Among individual models, the Wrangler carries the largest premium.
Where the Model Fails
Very cheap listings are predicted too high. 82% of the listings priced under $2,000 are predicted at more than double their asking price, against 4% of all other listings. These are likely cars with problems the data does not record, or down payments typed in as the price.
Robust Depreciation Rate
Refitting inside each brand tier gives almost the same age effect (−6.6% per year for both Standard and Luxury brands). Mileage costs more for luxury brands (−5.2% per 10,000 miles against −3.1%).
Limitations & Caveats
Missing Condition Data
The model's primary limitation is the absence of vehicle condition information (scratches, accident history, mechanical state) — a key driver of real-world price variation.
Asking Price Bias
The dataset contains listed asking prices, not actual transaction prices, introducing an upward bias that may not reflect real market-clearing values.
Model Baseline
At R² = 0.58, the linear model is a reasonable explanatory baseline but leaves predictive power on the table. Non-linear models (e.g., Gradient Boosting) are the natural next step.
🏁 Key Takeaways
01
Vehicle age is the #1 price driver. Each extra year lowers the asking price by about 6.8%, and every 10,000 miles by about 3.6%.
02
Diesel and 4WD vehicles are listed higher at every mileage and model year, largely because they are trucks and SUVs.
03
Age matters more than the badge. A 2020s Honda is listed well above a 1990s BMW.
04
R² = 0.58 is a baseline, not a ceiling. Condition and trim level are missing from the data, and prices are asking prices.
